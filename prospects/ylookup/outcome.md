# Ylookup — Outcome

## Result

Closed, lost. The UDINA demo and pitch landed well enough to **book a call with the CEO (Daniel Fraai)**. The call did not convert: Fraai did not want to outsource and preferred to **hire the engineer directly** — an FTE arrangement, not the Kaide Labs FDE engagement. That was not the outcome Kaide Labs wanted. A follow-up **pilot-sprint proposal** sent a couple of days later received **no response**. Relationship status: dormant, but warm — the founder's preference was to acquire the talent, not a rejection of the work.

## Why this outcome

The failure mode was **engagement-model mismatch, not demo quality.** The demo did its job — it proved technical execution to a non-technical, domain-expert founder. But the founder's response to "this person can build what we can't" was "then we should hire this person," not "we should retain their firm." This is structurally predictable in hindsight given the founder profile: Fraai values demonstrated, asymmetric solo execution and Ylookup has **no in-house engineering** (the "neither of us can write code" code gap). For that founder shape, a compelling solo-built demo reads as a **hiring signal**, and the FDE-sidecar framing competes against the simpler, cheaper-feeling instinct to just bring the builder in-house.

The cold pilot follow-up is consistent with that read: once the founder's frame is "hire, not engage," a pilot-sprint proposal is answering a question he is no longer asking.

## Generalizable learnings

1. **Demo quality is necessary but not sufficient.** A demo that lands can still lose if the engagement model doesn't fit how the founder wants to acquire capability. Qualify the engagement model *before* the demo proves the talent.
2. **Solo-execution proof can backfire into hire-intent.** With code-gap founders especially, an individually-attributed build can trigger "acquire the person" over "retain the firm." Where the engagement (not just the demo) is the product, position the *team/system/repeatability* — not the lone builder — or the win condition inverts.
3. **Code-gap founders are a double-edged ICP.** The same gap that makes the pitch land (they can't build it themselves) makes hiring the obvious alternative to outsourcing. Screen for whether a prospect is structurally a buyer-of-engagements vs. a buyer-of-employees.
4. **The riba-filter learning is the most durable artifact.** This engagement should never have reached the build stage under current rules: Ylookup's customer workflows (NAV reconciliation, LBO diligence, QoE on debt-financed deals) are structurally riba-entangled, and a riba-entangled customer base is now a **kill-at-sourcing** trigger — before intelligence, demo, or outreach. This is a pre-filter engagement (2026-03-16), preserved like Tracelight for the architecture, not the prospect. The most cost-effective version of this entire engagement was the one that never started. Reinforces the Tracelight-hardened filter: **filter at sourcing, not after the build.**
